Not All Thinking Helps

Which Reasoning Behaviors Predict Correctness?

Jean de Dieu Nyandwi, Leena Mathur, Yonatan Bisk, Graham Neubig
Carnegie Mellon University

Thinking models amplify visible deliberation, but not the behaviors most associated with correctness.

Abstract

We ask whether reasoning-oriented "thinking" training amplifies the behaviors that are actually associated with correct answers. Across 15 open-weight LLMs and VLMs, 6 benchmarks, and 15,282 annotated reasoning traces, we separate behavioral prevalence from behavioral value using Behavioral Lift: how much correctness changes when a behavior appears in a trace. We find a consistent mismatch. Thinking training strongly amplifies self-correction, hypothesis testing, and uncertainty acknowledgment, while the highest-lift behaviors are confidence calibration, knowledge alignment, and self-awareness. The result: traces can look more deliberative without becoming more reliable.

Key Findings

Amplification is not lift

Amplification mismatch

Thinking training amplifies self-correction, hypothesis testing, and uncertainty acknowledgment, but leaves confidence calibration and knowledge alignment largely unchanged.

Highest-lift behaviors

Confidence calibration has the strongest lift: +72.2% for VLMs and +79.6% for LLMs. Knowledge alignment is also high: +53.7% for VLMs and +80.3% for LLMs.

Calibration is not hedging

Uncertainty acknowledgment is amplified by thinking training, but has negative lift: −16.1% for VLMs and −13.9% for LLMs. A model saying "maybe" is not the same as a calibrated model.

Thinking helps through recovery

Thinking models recover 2–3× better on extended-reasoning tasks: VisualPuzzles 23.0% vs 8.4%, MATH-500 40.8% vs 17.8%, MMLU-Pro 16.8% vs 6.3%. But on LogiQA2, instruct models recover better: 24.5% vs 11.1%.

Prevalence

What thinking training amplifies

Thinking models produce far more self-correction, hypothesis testing, and uncertainty acknowledgment than instruct models. But prevalence alone says little about value.

Behavioral Lift

Which behaviors predict correctness

Behavioral Lift measures how much correctness changes when a behavior is present vs. absent. The most amplified behaviors are not the ones with the highest lift.

Recovery

Thinking helps most through recovery

Thinking models excel when tasks reward extended computation and recovery from intermediate mistakes. On pattern-matching tasks, instruct models can perform comparably.

Metrics

How we measure

Behavioral Lift

Lift(b) = P(correct | b = true) − P(correct | b = false)

Behavioral Lift separates behaviors that are frequent from behaviors that are useful. Positive lift means responses with the behavior are more likely to be correct; negative lift means the behavior is associated with lower accuracy. Lift is descriptive, not causal.

Recovery Rate

Recovery = P(correct | any failure mode = true)

Recovery Rate measures whether a model can still reach the correct answer after a detected reasoning failure.

Scaling

The pattern persists at scale

The amplification–lift mismatch holds across model sizes. Scaling does not close the gap.

Improving reasoning models requires more than making them think longer. Future training and evaluation should reward calibrated confidence, grounded use of evidence, appropriate domain knowledge, and recovery when reasoning goes wrong.
